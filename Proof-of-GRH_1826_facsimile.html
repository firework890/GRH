CONSPECTUS

A comparison of two bounds for the covolume of theta lifts yields a one-sided estimate for a smoothed sum over the powers of primes, and from that estimate the generalized hypothesis of Riemann for the functions L(s, χ) of Dirichlet follows. For the upper bound we use the divisibility of a family of lifts which nearly spans the space in which it lies. For the lower we choose, at each finite place, a twist whose first powers satisfy at once the bounds of Selmer and of Hecke, and compare the determinants which result upon the original lattices. The product formula then compares the two bounds upon one and the same family, and the theorem of prime numbers for a fixed modulus supplies the estimate which the criterion of the zeros requires.

It is proper to say at the outset in what order the argument proceeds. The first section fixes the remainder of the powers of primes and the spaces of forms among which the comparison is made. The second states the hypotheses under which the construction is performed, and the third the four propositions upon which everything rests, together with the deduction of the theorem from them. The sections which follow specify the parameters, construct the short family of lifts, and establish the two bounds; the appendices contain the local computations upon which those sections depend. The table placed at the end of this extract gives the disposition of the whole memoir.

§ 1. Introduction

The functions which Dirichlet attaches to the characters of a modulus possess, as is well known, an Euler product and a functional equation; and the situation of their zeros governs the distribution of the primes in an arithmetic progression, in the same manner as the zeros of the function ζ govern the distribution of all the primes. The hypothesis is that no zero of L(s, χ) lies in the half-plane ℜs > ½, so that every zero within the strip 0 < ℜs < 1 lies upon the middle line. We establish this here for every character and every modulus.

Theorem I. — For every Dirichlet character χ, every zero of L(s, χ) in the strip 0 < ℜs < 1 lies upon the line ℜs = ½. In fact L(s, χ) ≠ 0 for ℜs > ½, except that for the principal character the enunciation excepts its pole at s = 1.

The demonstration compares two bounds for the squared covolume of one and the same family of theta lifts, a family which nearly spans the space. The upper bound proceeds from the divisibility of the lifts along a chain of integral forms; its arithmetic term contains the difference of two smoothed remainders of the powers of primes, together with a correction at the smaller scale. For the lower bound we choose, at each finite embedding, a single anticyclotomic twist whose first m powers satisfy both the estimates of Selmer for the length and the normalized bounds for the values of Hecke. All these powers are taken at the level prescribed by the conductor of the original twist.

The estimates of Selmer govern the finite denominators of a Gram determinant formed upon the original lattice of Hecke; the bounds for the values enter the comparison of the volumes of the individual lifts. A weighted identity for the volumes along the chain, and a controlled completion of the short family, permit the product formula to be applied to one and the same system of vectors. The main term, being common to the two bounds, cancels; and the bound for the remainder of the powers of primes gains thereby a factor m−1, with m ≈ x1/2. The theorem of prime numbers for a fixed modulus bounds the remainders at the smaller scale, and there remains a one-sided estimate at the larger.

A quadratic twist then separates the poles of the logarithmic derivatives in the Mellin transform which results; the lemma of Landau excludes zeros to the right of the critical line; and the functional equation gives the assertion within the strip. It is to be added that the estimates are uniform neither in the modulus nor in the character, and are not required to be: each character is treated by itself, the fixed modulus q0 = 18480q being chosen after it.

A word is due upon the means employed, since they are not those usual in questions of this kind. The comparison is not made between two expressions for the same integral, but between two determinations of one and the same covolume: an arithmetic determination, which counts the lattice up to divisibility, and a geometric one, which measures the fundamental domain. That the two must agree is the content of the product formula; that they can be made to agree closely enough is the content of the four propositions which follow. The theta lift supplies the object upon which both determinations are made, and the twist supplies the freedom by which the geometric side is made small.

Disposition of the memoir

The remainder of the powers of primes and the spaces of forms are fixed in § 2. The four propositions, and the deduction of the theorem from them, occupy § 3; the choice of the ramification prime, the cancellation of the main term, and the estimate at the second scale are given there in order. The passage to all characters is made in § 4, and the criterion of the zeros is established in § 5. The parameters, the spaces, and the measures are specified in §§ 6–7 of the complete memoir; the short family is constructed, and the upper bound proved, in §§ 8–10; the estimates for the twists, the comparison at the finite places upon the original lattice, and the lower bound are established in §§ 11–13. The appendices contain the differential identities, the estimates of the open cell, the local comparisons, the crystalline bounds, the descent to the integral lattice, and the construction of the Galois representations.

§ 2. The Remainder of the Powers of Primes

Let q0 be a positive multiple of 18480, and let d be a prime congruent to q0/2 − 1 modulo q0. Then d ≡ 7 (mod 8), and d is prime to q0; for (q0/2 − 1)2 ≡ 1 modulo q0, and 8 divides q0/2. We write K = ℚ(√−d) and χd(n) = (n/d), the symbol of Legendre being extended by zero upon the multiples of d. For q = 1 or q = q0 we put

λq(n) = 1n≡1 (q) Λ(n)(1 − χd(n)), (1)

where Λ is the function of von Mangoldt; and for y ≥ 1

Rq(y) = Σn≤y λq(n)(1n − 1y) − log yφ(q) − cq, (2)

the number cq being the real part, at s = 1, of the function Gq(s) which is formed from the logarithmic derivatives of the completed functions L♮(s, ψχd) and L♮(s, ψ), summed over the characters ψ of modulus q.1 That the logarithmic derivatives written are regular at s = 1 follows from the non-vanishing of Dirichlet at 1, and from the simple pole in the principal case.

The comparison of covolumes evaluates this remainder at two scales, x and x1, with log x1 = xε/6 and log b = xε. Here b is the parameter of size, and x increases indefinitely while q0, d and ε remain fixed.

From these definitions there follows an exact identity, by which the weighted powers of primes are distributed among the places of the field E = K(ζN1). Multiplication by −n sends the set A bijectively upon the residue class of −n above q0; there are |B| choices of τ in A for which the translate lies in B when n ≡ 1 modulo q0, and none otherwise. The identity which results is

R = a( |B| Rq0(x) − Rq0(x1) + |A| R1(x1)). (3)

It is the exactness of this identity which is used below, and not merely an estimate; upon it depends the cancellation of the main term.

1. We write L♮(s, ψ) = (s−1)L(s, ψ) when ψ is principal, and L♮ = L otherwise, so that the function written is entire in either case.

2.1. Covolume and index

The automorphic part uses k = r + 1 − m, where r is a power of 3 in [x1/5, 3x1/5]. The source is a space of algebraic forms upon a definite unitary group of rank k. There are two split level primes g0, g1 and a split prime N; we write g = g0g1. At the chosen places v of E above g, the reduction of the principal level in rank r gives G = ∏v GLr(Fv). The target W, of dimension D, is the image of the orthogonal central idempotent e0 upon the principal-level forms of the first group U(Y0), of rank r, in the real rank-one projections specified in § 8.

The projector is a sum over tuples of finite types, indexed by square-free monic polynomials of degree r with non-zero constant term. Over each level prime at least one label is irreducible with a primitive root, and none of the labels may have a root in the residue field killed by the tame exponent Mω = (q0d)s+6. The rank-r group at the first step is the one upon ϱ = m/2 − 1 hyperbolic planes and the middle space of rank k + 1, with the split prime N upon its negative line.

The index of a system of vectors in W is measured with respect to the lattice which the integral structures of § 10 place upon it; the covolume is the square of the volume of the fundamental domain of that lattice, normalized as in § 7. It is a matter of some importance for what follows that the same lattice serves the arithmetic and the geometric side of the comparison: the divisibility estimates of § 10 and the theta comparisons of § 13 are made upon one and the same lattice, and the index is charged once in each argument of a single Gram pairing.

2.2. Notation and conventions

For q = 1 or q = q0, Rq(y) is the smoothed remainder (2). Upon the principal-level space V at K(g), the central idempotent e0 selects W = e0V. The composite Θϑ uses ϑj in the j-th lift, at the level prescribed by the original support of ϑ. The Haar measures of § 7 give volume one to the finite lattice stabilizers, and use the compact-dual normalization at the real places; quotients use counting measure upon the rational points. A definite class of stabilizer order ac has class-sum weight 1/ac, and each theta integral in the chain defining Θϑ is divided by the volume of its source level.

We use upright letters for the operators tr, ord and length, and italic letters for the variables; German letters, such as 𝔭, for the ideals; and the ordinary capital for the rings of integers and the lattices.

§ 3. The Four Propositions

The statements which follow are joint statements concerning one and the same construction. The bounds above and below refer to the same maps and to the same integral modules as the statement of existence; it is this, and nothing else, which allows the main term to cancel.

Propositio I. — Existence of the data of comparison. Let q0 and d be as above, and let 0 < ε < 1/100. For every sufficiently large real x the construction furnishes data satisfying

log b = xε,  log x1 = xε/6,  |A| ≤ |B| log b,
(106 log x)2 ≤ log x1, (4)

and whose target has positive dimension and common main term H with |H| + m|R| = o(b2). The ranks, the level, the family of maps, and the real numbers defined above belong to one such datum.

Propositio II. — A family of short lifts. For the data of Proposition I, some independent system y1, …, yn in W has index at most δ, satisfies n ≥ (1 − 4/b2)D, and is such that

Vσ(y) ≤ exp{n(H − mR + Bcov)}  (σ ∈ Aut ℂ). (5)

Propositio III. — Lower bound for the covolume. For the same data, let y1, …, yn be independent in W, with n ≥ (1 − 4/b2)D and index at most δ. If Vσ(y) ≤ eVup for every σ, then

D(H − 3Bcov) ≤ Vup + 2δ. (6)

Propositio IV. — Criterion of the zeros, one-sided. Let 8 divide q0, let a0 be a unit class modulo q0 with a0 ≡ 7 (mod 8), and let β > ½. Suppose that for every prime d ≡ a0 (mod q0) one has

Rq0(x) ≤ xβ−1 (7)

for all sufficiently large real x, the threshold depending perhaps upon d. Then L♮(ρ, ψ) ≠ 0 for every ψ of modulus q0 and every ρ with ℜρ > β.

The first three propositions are proved together, by the construction of the short family and the two comparisons of covolume; the fourth is a criterion, and its proof is independent of the rest. We give in the following sections the deduction of the theorem from the four, and such indications concerning their demonstration as may serve to make the argument intelligible.

3.1. The choice of the ramification prime

The short family is made short by a penalty a log N at each place of A. Put

Hrow = H + mΣτ∈A (J(a) − P(a, τ)) − |A| a log N. (8)

This is HN in § 6.3 of the memoir. Using log N ≥ λ, that calculation gives Hrow ≤ H − mR + B, and the same section shows that |H| + m|R| = o(b2), which is the second assertion of Proposition I. The single rational scaling at all places, which the short family admits, is what makes the comparison possible; the rank estimate of § 8 and the divisibility estimates of § 10 enter upon its unscaled and its scaled images respectively.

3.2. Cancellation of the main term

Take the system of Proposition II and put h = H − mR + Bcov. Proposition III, applied with Vup = nh, gives D(H − 3Bcov) ≤ nh + 2δ; independence gives n ≤ D, and Proposition I gives −h ≤ o(1). The cost of the dimensions missing from the system is therefore bounded also when h < 0, namely

nh = Dh + (D − n)(−h) ≤ Dh + 4Dx−99, (9)

and with the bound for the index this yields

D(H − 3Bcov) ≤ D(H − mR + Bcov) + 4Dx−99
+ 10D log(1 + b2)B. (10)

In particular mR is bounded by a fixed multiple of Bcov, and the estimate R ≤ x−1/2+6ε follows at the scale x. It is to be observed that the calculation uses the sign neither of R nor of H; this is the whole advantage of the one-sided form, and the reason why no hypothesis upon the size of R at the smaller scale is required. For the last inequality one divides by Bcov; since a ≥ xb and f ≥ 1, the ratio of x−99 to Bcov tends to zero, while the ratio of log(1 + b2) to log b tends to 2.

3.3. The estimate at the second scale

The normalization of Rq matters here. Let

Eq(t) = Σn≤t λq(n) − t/φ(q). (11)

For fixed q, d and every J > 0, the theorem of prime numbers in progressions gives

Indeed, apply Siegel–Walfisz to the unit classes modulo qd, which is fixed; the class modulo q is 1. § 4. The Passage to All Characters

Let χ be a character of modulus q ≥ 1, and put q0 = 18480q. Induce χ to a character ψ of modulus q0. The two Euler products differ only by the factors

1 − χ(p)p−s,  p | q0,  p ∤ q, (14)

and each of these is non-zero in ℜs > 0, since |χ(p)p−s| < 1 there. The equality of the products therefore extends by continuation from the half-plane of convergence to the region required.

If ℜρ > ½, take β with ½ < β < min{1, ℜρ}, and ε with 0 < ε < min{1/100, (β − ½)/6}. The bound at the second scale, which is the assertion of Proposition IV for the class q0/2 − 1, gives Rq0(x) ≤ xβ−1 eventually, for every prime d in that class. Proposition IV then excludes a zero of L♮(s, ψ) at ρ, and hence a zero of L(s, χ) at the same point; and since β may be taken arbitrarily close to ½, no zero lies in ℜs > ½.

For a primitive character of conductor q′, the completed function within the strip 0 < ℜs < 1 is

(q′/π)(s+aχ)/2 Γ((s + aχ)/2) L(s, χ), (15)

where (−1)aχ = χ(−1) and aχ ∈ {0, 1}. This function has the same zeros as L within the strip, and the functional equation reflects a zero at ρ into a zero at 1 − ρ; the relation holds also for the trivial primitive character of conductor 1, whose poles lie outside the open strip. The result in the half-plane therefore excludes zeros to the left of the middle line as well. For an imprimitive character the Euler factors which relate it to its primitive inducer are again non-zero throughout the open strip, so that nothing is lost in the passage from ψ to χ.

Thus Theorem I follows from Propositions I to IV, and the proof is complete.

§ 5. The Criterion of the Zeros

It remains to justify Proposition IV, upon which the passage to all characters depends. For β ≥ 1 the assertion follows from the convergent Euler products in ℜs > 1; we may therefore assume β < 1. The office of the quadratic twist is to separate the poles of the sum of logarithmic derivatives, and the first step is to secure the non-vanishing which that separation requires.

Fix a modulus q, a real T0 ≥ 0, and ½ < β < 1. For any unit class of primes d ≡ 7 (mod 8) modulo q, there exists d in that class such that all the functions L(s, ψχd), for ψ of modulus q, are non-zero in ℜs ≥ β, |ℑs| ≤ T0. Let ψ0 be the primitive inducer of a fixed ψ; the twist of ψ0 by χd is primitive, since the two conductors are coprime, and the extra Euler factors of ψχd have no zeros in ℜs > 0. It suffices therefore to bound the exceptional primes for the primitive twists of each of the finitely many ψ0.

NOTAE

The four propositions are not independent of one another, and the reader who would follow the demonstration in order will do well to observe the following points.

i.  The data of Proposition I are not constructed all at once. The primes g0, g1 and N are fixed first, then the level, then the twist at each finite place; the estimates show that each choice may be made so that the conditions already imposed are not disturbed. This is the meaning of the phrase for every sufficiently large x: the thresholds depend upon q0, d and ε, but not upon the character.

ii.  The index δ of Proposition II measures how far the short family is from a basis of W; it enters Proposition III through the comparison of the Gram determinant with the volume of the fundamental domain, and it is there that the hypothesis n ≥ (1 − 4/b2)D is used. No deeper property of the family is required at that point.

iii.  Proposition IV is one-sided by design. A two-sided estimate for Rq0 would give the same conclusion, but no such estimate follows from the comparison of covolumes, the geometric side furnishing a lower bound only. The quadratic twist, and the choice of the class a0 ≡ 7 (mod 8), serve to separate the poles which the passage to all characters would otherwise leave in the way.

iv.  The numbering of the equations is that of the memoir; the numbers (1) to (19) correspond to the order of their first occurrence, and are not resumed in this extract.
